Image Steganography

Image steganography conceals secret data directly within the pixels of a digital picture without noticeably changing its appearance. It typically works by slightly altering the color values of individual pixels, tucking the secret message into parts of the image to which the human eye is least sensitive. To any casual observer, the resulting image looks absolutely identical to the original file.

  • Least Significant Bit (LSB) pixel manipulation
  • Color channel and frequency domain modifications
  • Visually identical to the original image file

Video Steganography

Video steganography embeds covert messages across the moving frames and synchronized audio tracks of a digital video file. Because a video is essentially a rapid sequence of images combined with sound, data can be scattered randomly across thousands of frames or hidden within the motion patterns. This vast amount of cover data easily swallows the secret message without causing any visible glitches or lag.

  • Spatial frame distribution and motion vector embedding
  • Combined audio track and visual layer manipulation
  • Plays smoothly with no visual or structural glitches

Audio Steganography

Audio steganography hides secret information within the sound waves of a digital audio track while maintaining its original acoustic quality. This is often achieved by embedding data into imperceptible background noise or by slightly shifting the phase of the sound wave itself. Anyone listening to the track will hear only the normal song or recording, completely unaware of the hidden payload.

  • Phase shifting and inaudible frequency masking
  • Least Significant Bit (LSB) audio wave encoding
  • Maintains flawless original playback and sound quality

Text Steganography

Text steganography is the technique of hiding a secret message inside ordinary, everyday text so that nobody even suspects it exists. It often works by inserting special "zero-width" characters into the text, which computers can detect but human eyes cannot see. Data can also be hidden by adding unnoticeable extra blank spaces between words or at the ends of sentences to represent hidden binary codes.

  • Zero-width space code embedding
  • Whitespace and punctuation variation keys
  • Indistinguishable in standard word editors
